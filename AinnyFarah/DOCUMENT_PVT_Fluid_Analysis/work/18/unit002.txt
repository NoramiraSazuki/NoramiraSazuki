Gentlemen
Surface samples were collected from the subject well Dulang B-12 and were forwarded to our laboratory for use in a reservoir fluid study. Presented in the following report are the results of the study as requested by Petronas Carigali Sdn Bhd
Upon arrival in the laboratory, the following preliminary quality checks were performed on the samples. The opening pressure of the separator gas cylinder was determined at separator temperature. The bubble point pressure of the separator liquids were determined at separator temperature, based on these results the more representative samples were chosen for analyses
The composition of the separator gas was analysed through heptanes plus by gas chromatography, the separator gas was also analysed for oxygen, this quality check is performed to determine whether the gas sample was contaminated with air. The composition of the liquid was analysed to heptanes plus by flash/chromatographic technique, the measured physical properties of the heptanes plus together with the compositional data of the separator samples and calculated well stream are reported on page four
The separator gas from Cylinder No 1209-A and liquid from Cylinder No WIB-7417 were then physically recombined to the producing gas-liquid ratio of 240 standard cubic feet of separator gas per barrel of separator liquid at 112&quot;F and a portion of the reservoir fluid was charged to a high pressure visual cell preheated to the reservoir temperature of 200’F. The fluid was then subjected to a constant composition expansion at 200°F during which the fluid was measured to have a bubble point pressure of 1367 psig which is lower than the reported reservoir pressure of 1727 psig at reservoir temperature of 200‘F. This data was reported to a representative of Petronas Carigali Sdn Bhd., who requested us to adjust the recombined reservoir fluid so that it has a Bubble point pressure equal to the gas-oil contact pressure at reservoir temperature

The Partial pressure volume relations measurements of the recombined reservoir fluid can be found on page five
The recombined reservoir fluid was then charged into a high pressure visual cell and then gas is added to increase the reservoir fluid bubble point pressure to the gas-oil contact pressure of 1668 psig at 200‘F. This adjusted reservoir fluid was used for subsequent analyses
The composition of the adjusted reservoir fluid was then analysed to heptanes plus by flash/chromatographic technique, the measured physical properties of heptanes plus together with the compositional analyses are reported on page six
A known volume of the adjusted reservoir fluid was charged to a high pressure visual cell and then thermally expanded to the reservoir temperature of 200’F. The sample was observed while constant composition expansion pressure-volume relations were being performed. During these tests, the fluid was found to exhibit a bubble-point pressure of 1668 psig which confirm that the reservoir fluid was adjusted correctly. The volumetric data and pressure volume relation measurements of the reservoir fluid can be found on page seven and eight respectively
Subsequently the reservoir fluid was subjected to a differential pressure depletion at the reservoir temperature of 200’F. The fluid evolved a total of 410 cubic feet of gas at 14.73 psia and 60’F per barrel of residual oil at 60’F. The associated relative oil volume factor was 1.249 barrels of saturated fluid per barrel of residual oil. The oil densities and composition of the evolved gases were measured at each point during the differential pressure depletion also compositional analysis was performed on the residual oil and this data is included in the summary of the differential depletion data on page nine, the compositional of the evolved gases and residual oil is reported on pages ten to eleven
The gas in solution and oil relative volume data obtained from the differential pressure depletion study has been adjusted to separator conditions, this data is tabulated on pages eighteen and nineteen and graphically presented on pages twenty two to twenty five and twenty seven to thirty
The viscosity of the reservoir fluid was measured over a wide range of pressures at 200’F in a rolling ball viscosimeter. The viscosity of ,the fluid was found to vary from a minimum of 0.635 centipoises at the bubble point pressure of 1668 psig to a maximum of 1.290 centipoises at atmospheric pressure. The results of the viscosity measurements together with the calculated gas viscosity are tabulated on page thirteen

The reservoir fluid was subjected to a series of single-stage separator test at 112°F to determine the effect of separator pressure on stock tank oil gravity, formation volume factor and gas-oil ratio. The results of the single-stage separator test are presented on page fourteen
The evolved gas from the pressure stages of separation from the single stage separator test were collected in the laboratory and analysed through heptanes plus by gas chromatography, the composition of the separator gases to heptanes plus are tabulated on pages fifteen
The stock tank liquid obtained from the single stage separator tests were analysed to heptanes plus and are reported on pages sixteen
