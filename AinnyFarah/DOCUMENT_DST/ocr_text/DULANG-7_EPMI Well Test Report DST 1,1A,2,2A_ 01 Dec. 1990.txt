DULANG 7 PRODUCTION TEST RESULTS

SUMMARY

Two production tests, each consisting of two stages, were successfully
and safely conducted at Dulang 7 well. The additional Stage entails
perforating additional zone/zones for a  commingle test. The
objectives of the tests were to determine fluid type, measure the
well’s productivity, evaluate reservoir properties, and obtain fluid
samples. Test 1 was conducted on the E-34/36 reservoirs and 1A was a
commingled test of the E-34/36 with the E-27 and E-26 reservoirs.
Test 2 was on the E-6 reservoir and Test 2A was a commingled test. of
the E-6 with the E-13 reservoir. Each test averaged about 3 days with
minima] trouble and downtime. All perforations were made at
underbalance conditions with brine used as the load fluid for Test 1
while diesel was used for Test 2. Test 2 required swabbing. None of
the zones tested had any H2S present in the gas stream. The drilled
hole was vertical and the tests were performed on a drill ship (Chris
Chenery).

Test 1(E-34/36 Zone)

This zone which was perforated between 1425.0 -1430.0 m RKB produced
at an average rate of 763 STBPD of 39.29 API oil, no water, and 437
SCF/STB solution GOR through 3/8-inch choke. The FWHP was 448 psig and
the FBHP was 1546.9 psia at 1420 m RKB. The waxy oi] had a pour point
of 909 F and was dark brown in color. The gas stream contained about
60 % C02. The zone’s productivity is estimated to be 1.71 STBPD/psi.
The initial formation pressure from the buildup test is 1993.3 psia at
1420.0 m RKB. This corresponds to a pressure of 1999.6 psia @ 1426.2
m, based on a measured wellbore gradient of 0.31 psi/ft which is in
excellent agreement with that from the RFT, 1998.9 psia @ 1426.2m.
The maximum recorded BHT was 2229 F. The effective permeability to

TEST_PROCEDURE

With the well shut-in, each zone was perforated with Schlumberger’s
2-1/8 inch Enerjet through tubing perforating gun (6 spf. at zero
degrees phasing). To minimize formation damage, the zones were
perforated underbalance with differentials into the wellbore ranging
from 300 to 750 psi. These levels of differentials for Tests 1 and 2
were obtained by utilizing either brine or diesel. After perforating,
the well remained shut-in until the gun was retrieved and the
bottomhole pressure gauge assembly run back into the wellbore.

Surface opening and shut-in procedures were used.

The average test duration, including plug-back to prepare for the next
zone, was 3 days. Although the test design called for a typical dual
flow, dual shut-in test procedure, the initial flow and = shut-in
periods were omitted to minimize testing time since good measurements
of the initial pressures from RFTs were available. The major flow was
designed for about 6 to 12 hours so as to achieve sufficient cleanup
and stabilized flow conditions which are necessary for good assessment
of the well’s productivity and for collection of representative fluid
samples. Periodically, small amounts of surface samples were taken at
the choke manifold for on-site analysis which includes determination
of BS&W content, composition of the gas, API and gas gravity, pour
point, and Ci~ content of any produced water. Samples for detailed
laboratory analysis included duplicate sets of gas, oi], and water
from separator. A detail list of those collected are summarized in
Table 2. The pressurized oil samples were collected by mercury
displacement, while the gas was collected in evacuated cylinders.
Bottomhole samples were not collected from any of the zones as. their
sand quality was poor which will require high drawdowns and thus
rendering them potentially unrepresentative. .

TEST EQUIPMENT

A schematic of the bottomhole test assembly is shown in Figure 1. It
consists of a mule shoe, a nipple, seal assembly, a locator-sub, a
permanent packer, and a 3.5-inch tubing string. Figure 2 shows the
layout of the upper. test assembly and surface equipment. The upper
test assembly consists of Flopetrol’s sub-sea test-tree, sub-sea
lubricator valve, control header, wireline BOP, and a wireline
lubricator, The surface equipment consists of a choke manifold, a
diesel-fired heater, a three-phase separator, 2 50-barrel test tanks,
and a transfer pump. A 2 MBTU heater was used to heat the waxy crude
at the surface prior to routing to the separator. Flowlines to flare
had the option of being either routed to the port or starboard-side of
the floater.

Bottomhole pressures and temperatures were monitored with high
accuracy Flopetrol gauges. Two types of pressure gauges were used: a
Flopetrol’s quartz sensor (CRG) which has very good thermal transient
behavior, and a strain sensor (SSDP), which was used as a_ backup.
Readings from the quartz sensor were displayed in real-time and used
for all analysis. A sketch of the layout of the bottomhole pressure
gauge assembly is shown in Figure 3. Wellhead pressures were
monitored with a dead-weight tester, and with a Foxboro meter serving
as a backup which also records the- surface temperature.

DISCUSSION OF RESULTS

TEST I (E-34/36 Zone)

No major problems or downtime. losses occurred while testing this zone.
Brine was used as the load fluid to achieve a differential of
approximately 320 psi into the wellbore. Diesel, though preferred,
was not use as the load fluid for this test due to saving in rig time
(6 to 8 hours; period required for circulating out).

TEST 1A (E-34/36 + E-27 + E-26 Zones)

To achieve underbalance perforating conditions, the additional
perforations in the E-27 and E-28 sandstones were made while the well
was flowing. The approximate differential during perforation was
about 500 psi. A PLT survey was run after the well was cleaned-up.

Appendix B contains the details of the events and data collected
during this test.

TEST 2 (E-6 Zone)

This zone was perforated with diesel and air (about 13.4 barrels of
load diesel was pre-swabbed) cushions in the wellbore to provide
maximum safe differential, 750 psi. The zone was initially perforated
between 1236.0 -1242.0 m RKB. When the well was found to produce at
an estimated rate of only about 20 STBPD, the interval between 1234.0
-1243.0 m RKB was added/ reperforated to enhance fluid entry into the
wellbore. Note, that this revised perforation level was made nearer
to the overlying gas cap to gas lift the well. A major buildup was

which would require an extended buildup period. Average permeability
based on pseudo-steady-state flow calculations is estimated to be
about 13 md (for an assumed net pay of 5.0 m).

A detailed listing of the chronology of events and data collected
during this test period are included in Appendix C.

TEST 2A (E-6 + E-13 Zones)

The additional perforations in the E-13 zone was made while the well
was flowing to achieve underbalance perforation conditions. A PLT
survey was run at the end to determine the production split between
the zones. Gas entry was indicated to be between 1234.0 and 1236.0 m
RKB in the E-6 reservoir.

No buildups were run during the test period since the zones indicated
low permeabilities requiring extended shut-in period.

Details of the chronology of events and data collected during this
test are included in Appendix D.
